## Open limitations

### 2. No relevance threshold in retrieval

The retriever always returns `k` chunks, even for a question completely outside the domain
("what do you think about football" still populates the "Sources retrieved" table). The model
refuses correctly by prompt instruction, but the UI shows sources that do not ground anything.

Tested on 2026-08-25 (`notebooks/11_retrieval_benchmark.ipynb`, axis B) and **the test failed for
a technical reason**: the collection was built with `hnsw.space='l2'`, so LangChain's Euclidean
relevance function returns scores outside 0–1 (some negative) and a threshold of 0.2 discards
chunks regardless of relevance — 62.2% hit-rate against `similarity`'s 81.1%. That number
measures a broken metric, not a worse strategy, and is recorded that way in the notebook. Testing
this properly requires rebuilding the collection in cosine space.

### 5. PDF extraction is text-only — diagrams and nameplates are lost

`PyPDFLoader` extracts running text only. Wiring diagrams, schematics and nameplate photographs,
frequently the critical information in industrial maintenance, never enter the corpus. The single
most valuable thing the corpus is missing.

### 7. Corpus unbalanced between equipment families

~2,766 VFD pages vs ~913 electric-motor pages (~3:1), already corrected from an initial 18:1.
It can still bias "General question" retrieval (no machine selected) slightly towards VFDs.

### 11. Safety Data Sheet corpus still missing

The original design called for manuals **plus** safety data sheets for the lubricants, oils and
cleaning products used on the equipment. The safety-first contract was built without them
(rule 6 in `agent.py`, the live gate in `safety.py`), grounded in the safety sections the
motor/VFD manuals already contain. What remains open is the SDS corpus itself: precautions today
cover what the manuals say, not product-specific chemical safety.

### 12. The defect dataset is not specifically about motors or VFDs

The 4 curated MVTec AD categories (`cable`, `metal_nut`, `screw`, `transistor`) are components
physically plausible on or around a motor or drive, not photographs of motor or drive failures
(worn bearings, damaged windings, corroded terminal boxes, dust-clogged heat sinks, damaged
fans, melted connectors). How directly the classifier transfers to those is not established.
See opportunity 3.

### 13. The zero-shot vision baseline performs worse than the trivial baseline

Measured on the held-out split: zero-shot `gpt-4.1-mini` scores 42.5%, worse than always
predicting "good" (77.1%). It invents defects in photographs of undamaged parts (7% recall on
"good"). A pure zero-shot approach is not reliable for this task without a trained classifier.

### 15. Evaluation scoring is a keyword proxy, not human judgement

`evaluation.py::keyword_score()` checks whether a phrase appears literally in the answer: a
correct answer worded differently counts as a miss, a wrong answer repeating the keywords counts
as a hit. The 50% pass threshold was chosen once and never tuned. `source_score()` checks the
manual family, not the page — there is no human page-level ground truth for the 37 scenarios.
Mitigated, not eliminated: evidence keywords are always verified absent from the question, so
nothing scores by echo.

### 16. Self-preference bias in the safety judge

`safety.py::check_safety_precautions()` uses `gpt-4.1-mini` to judge answers generated by
`gpt-4.1-mini`. Mitigated by a second deterministic regex checker with no LLM call; the agreement
rate (94.6% on agent answers, 78.4% on baseline answers) is published as the confidence in the
judge's number rather than hidden.

### 17. LangSmith traces live in an account shared with unrelated projects

`tracing.py` isolates this project in a dedicated LangSmith project, which is separation at
project level, not account level. Three trace URLs in the committed outputs of
`notebooks/08_tracing_observability.ipynb` contain the organisation UUID. They are not
credentials and grant no access; they are evidence that the traces are real. Worth knowing before
publishing screenshots from the same account.

### 21. Two answers still fail the safety ordering after the live gate

Measured 2026-09-04 (`notebooks/10_evaluation_baseline.ipynb`, part 3b): the raw agent prompt
fails precaution ordering on 11 of 33 answers that recommend an action (33.3%); `enforce_safety()`
rewrites 10 of them, and **2 of 34 delivered answers still fail** (5.9%) — `GEN01` (a parameter
check with no precautions at all) and `GEN02` (precautions present but after the actions). The
rewrite is attempted once and re-checked once; a second rewrite pass, or `mode="block"` for
these cases, are the obvious next experiments. Reported as measured.

## Open opportunities

### 3. Real photographs of motor and VFD faults

The highest-impact improvement to the vision path: a genuinely representative set — worn or
corroded bearings, burnt windings, degraded insulation, overheated terminal boxes, dust-clogged
heat sinks, damaged fans, oxidised connectors.

Attempted once and **deliberately abandoned**: a candidate thermal-imaging dataset trained to
98.9%, but its 369 images turned out to be only **11 independent scenes** of near-identical frames
(neighbouring frames differing by 0.55–2.47 grey levels out of 255), with a single scene for three
of the four classes — so no split could measure generalisation at all. The cheap neighbouring-frame
test that exposed it is the reusable part. A second experiment, a component-identity classifier
at 100%, was dropped for the same reason: each category had its own capture background.

### 4. Audio — diagnosis from the sound of the motor or drive

Investigated 2026-08-19 and deferred. A real, domain-relevant dataset exists: **UOEMD-VAFCVS**
(University of Ottawa Electric Motor Dataset — Vibration and Acoustic Faults under Constant and
Variable Speed Conditions), Mendeley Data, DOI `10.17632/msxs4vj48g`: a real induction motor
driven by a real VFD, with microphone, accelerometers and temperature; faults include rotor
imbalance, misalignment, stator winding faults, broken rotor bars and bearing faults.

Why it is not as straightforward as MVTec was: only 128 recordings (needs augmentation or a
few-shot approach); multi-sensor CSVs at 42 kHz, not ready-to-use audio (extract the acoustic
channel, cut clips, spectrograms/MFCCs via `librosa` or `torchaudio`, neither in the project);
a coded label scheme (`H-H` healthy, `B-B` bearing…) to map to readable labels. When picking this
up: download and inspect first, confirm the label mapping, then write training code.

### 5. Rank past resolutions by effectiveness (the open half)

The zero-cost prior-occurrence panel is built (`recurrence.py`, see the table below) and
deliberately **reports without recommending**: on VFD-04's real data, `F30805` was answered twice
with a module replacement (and came back) and three times with a power cycle, so a frequency
ranking would recommend masking a hardware fault. `resolution_events.outcome`
(`resolved` | `temporary` | `not_resolved`) is now collected on every recorded resolution, but
there is not yet enough data to rank actions by whether they *held*. After months of use it
becomes possible to say "the module replacement held for 4 years; the reset held for 2 days" —
only then does a history-based recommendation have a foundation.

### 6. A knob to turn the reranker off

The reranker is the most optional LLM call on the critical path: +5.4 pp hit-rate and +0.085 MRR
for 7 s → 43 s of batch latency (`notebooks/11`). Every other expensive behaviour is switchable
through a `FACTORY_FLOOR_*` setting; this one is hard-coded `rerank=True` in
`services.build_diagnostic_retriever`. A `Settings` field threaded through that call is all it
takes, and the benchmark justifying either choice already exists.
